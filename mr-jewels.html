II.2

What the history shows

Patterns found by re-reading the full history, not just the months I ran. Each is labelled: measured pattern, correlation, anomaly or seasonality.

Correlation, not causation

February 2026 already showed the August 'wider, not better' pattern; orders sit near a 2,000 a month ceiling

Jan to Feb 2026: sessions +39.5%, orders -4.4%, conversion rate 0.687% to 0.471%. Aug 2026 landed on the same point: 4,20,817 sessions, 1,960 orders, 0.466%, versus Feb's 4,18,236, 1,970, 0.471%. Orders stayed in 1,642-2,060 from Oct 2025 while sessions swung 2,36,079-4,20,817. AOV and conversion move opposite ways: AOV +14.0% Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, conversion -42.0%, gross sales per session ₹38.6 to ₹25.4. Aug 2026 vs Aug 2025: orders +58.6% (1,236 to 1,960) on Google billed x6.48 (₹1,67,886 to ₹10,87,899), about ₹1,271 of extra Google billing per extra order, Google only; Meta Aug 2026 unknown.

The numbers

Sessions vs conversion rate r = -0.93 (n=12); sessions vs orders r = +0.74; log-log slope 0.37 (1% more sessions, ~0.37% more orders); AOV vs conversion r = -0.55; Feb 2026 and Aug 2026 gross sales ₹1.22 Cr vs ₹1.07 Cr on the same orders. Revenue YoY not comparable: Aug 2025 is workbook net (₹56.0 L), Aug 2026 is Shopify gross (₹1.07 Cr) or derived net (₹80.2 L). AOV in the series is net of discounts (Aug: (10,684,206-531,024)/1,960 = ₹5,180).

So whatExtra traffic has hit the same ceiling twice. The cart, not reach, caps orders; Feb tracking break (2.9% coverage) means the cause of Feb's dip cannot be fully separated.

Seasonality

The spring demand dip is seasonal; spend did not flex with it in year two

Oct-Jan vs Mar-Jun, using actual months only. Year one: spend -18.1%, orders -17.0%, CAC flat. Year two: spend +6.0%, orders -11.6%, CAC +19.9%. Only Meta rose in year two (+14.2%); Google fell 17.9%.

The numbers

Y1 (Oct24-Jan25 vs Mar-Jun25): orders 3,399 to 2,822; spend ₹39.9 L to ₹32.7 L; CAC ₹1,174 to ₹1,158; MER 3.34x to 2.94x. Y2 (Oct25-Jan26 vs Mar-Jun26): orders 7,802 to 6,900; spend ₹82.9 L to ₹87.9 L; CAC ₹1,062 to ₹1,274; net revenue ₹3.32 Cr to ₹3.15 Cr; MER 4.01x to 3.58x. Meta ₹61.8 L to ₹70.6 L; Google ₹21.1 L to ₹17.3 L.

So whatPart of the 2026 MER and CAC slide is calendar, part is spend held flat into a weaker quarter. Plan spend by season. Two observations only (two years); Oct-Jan includes festive and wedding peaks.

Correlation, not causation

CAC rose 77% in 11 months and the rise sits almost entirely in Meta spend per order

Blended CAC ₹903 (Aug 2025) to ₹1,597 (Jul 2026). Of the ₹694 rise, ₹570 (82%) is Meta spend per order (₹788 to ₹1,357); Google spend per order moved ₹115 to ₹240 and shows no trend from Oct onward (₹193-362). Meta share of spend fell 93.9% to 78.4% year on year, but Meta spend still doubled (₹90.4 L to ₹1.91 Cr) while Google grew x8.96 (₹5.9 L to ₹52.6 L).

The numbers

Meta Feb to Jun 2026: spend +21.0%, orders -9.6%. Meta spend vs orders r = +0.17 and Google spend vs orders r = +0.71 (Sep 2025-Jul 2026, n=11). Google share of blended spend peaked 31.7% (Nov 2025) and was 15.0% in Jul 2026. Jul 2026 orders (1,538) are the workbook estimate.

So whatThe Google rebuild addressed the smaller cost line. Orders are all-channel, so this is spend allocation per order, not Meta-attributed CAC; Meta results by campaign were never supplied.

Seasonality

March return spike repeats; the Aug 2025 MER peak was flattered by a low return rate

March return rate 35.6% (2025) and 29.4% (2026) against 21.6% and 17.6% for the other 11 months of each year. Net revenue fell 31.4% and 25.4% Feb to Mar while orders fell only 17.7% and 7.9%. February did not repeat (33.5% in 2025, 15.8% in 2026). MER fell in two steps, not gradually: 5.02x (Aug 2025) to 4.05x (Sep), a six-month plateau of 3.82-4.17x (avg 4.04x), then 3.43x (Mar 2026) and 2.68x (Jul 2026). Q3 to Q4 2025 was already -12.4% (4.51x to 3.95x).

The numbers

Order-weighted return rate 22.6% (Aug24-Jul25) to 18.9% (Aug25-Jul26); 19.2% excluding the estimated Jul 2026. Aug 2025 return rate 10.1% is the 24-month low; at 19.83% (average of other year-two months) Aug 2025 MER is ~4.48x, not 5.02x. Mar 2025 net revenue per order ₹2,860 is the 24-month low. MER vs monthly spend r = -0.69 (Aug25-Jul26, n=12) vs -0.15 across 24 months.

So whatReturns lag and post in batches (days 18-31), so month-level MER is noisy. Compare quarters and expect a March return hit in 2027. Workbook return-rate definition is not stated and may differ from Shopify's 19.8% reversals.

Measured pattern

The Search layer got cheaper while its share of Google spend shrank 5x

Search CPA fell ₹117 (Q2 2025) to ₹17 (Q2 2026), CPC ₹5.70 to ₹0.69, CTR 28% to 39% (a brand-term profile). Search cost fell 21.6% (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) while total Google cost rose x3.94, so Search went from 1.05% to 0.21% of Google cost. Non-search CPA rose ₹969 to ₹1,489 (+53.7%). Search conversions peaked in Q1 2026 (242.6) and fell 32.4% in Q2 (164.0). Q3 2026 CPA ₹165 is the new brand campaign: ₹12,811 for 8.00 conversions (₹1,601); the rest of Search ran ₹3,697 for 91.98 conversions, ₹40.2 (vs ₹17.2 in Q2).

The numbers

Quarterly Search CPA: ₹116.8, 32.1, 34.9, 20.5, 17.2, then 165.1 (Q3 2026, to 17 Aug, partial). Search share of conversions 24.3% (Q3 2025) to 15.3% (Q2 2026) on 0.21% of cost. Overview-export basis, all-action conversions.

So whatBrand demand (~₹64 a day) is a ceiling, not a growth lever; the cheap Search CPA could not absorb budget. My brand campaign explains the Q3 spike, but the inherited layer also worsened 2.3x.

II.3

What ads can't fix, and what the data can't prove

Not a marketing problem

What the data shows ads cannot fix. Saying so early saves budget.

  • Returns: ₹2.41 Cr reversed in 12 months (19.8% of gross); about ₹21.2 L in August vs a ₹10.88 L Google bill. fastrrv3 reverses at 23.8% vs 6.7% native on 75.8% of sales; deity and animal pendants 40-63%, openable kadas 7-15%. RTO vs customer-return split unknown.
  • Cart abandonment: cart-to-order 16.8% to 13.5%; 1,43,983 carts added in a year, 1,23,133 left. At 25% cart-to-order the store does 98.6 orders a day on today's traffic; buying 100 a day costs ₹45-52 L a month.
  • Margin unknown: COGS never supplied. Break-even margin on August's extra spend 33.4% (gross) to 44.6% (net); at ₹29,000/day Stage 5 breaks even at 30%. No campaign can be called profitable.
  • Meta spend: 78.4% of ad spend and 2.26 M sessions, but Aug-Sep 2026 spend never supplied; Oct 2025 monthly sheet vs daily tracker conflicts by ₹55,794.
  • Billing: manual ₹15,000 netbanking top-ups; two blackouts in August (10 Aug about ₹32,000 lost); ₹18,571 left on 1 Sep vs ₹33,000/day.
  • Catalogue and stock: 8,396 SKUs, 478 movers; 2,153 zero-stock variants live; 1,753 variants (34.7%) sold nothing holding 4,574 units; 531 SKUs advertised out of stock; 4.0 months cover.
  • Checkout and attribution: headless checkouts strip the referrer, so only 3.4% of orders carry a source and 93% of Shopify revenue sits under 'None'; Shopify reports 0.01% conversion vs 0.466% true.
  • Orders ceiling: 1,642-2,060 a month since Oct 2025 across 2,36,079-4,20,817 sessions; spring dip repeated in both years (Mar-Jun orders -17.0% in year one, -11.6% in year two).
  • Repeat purchase: 79.6% new customers, month-1 repeat 12.6% decaying to ~1% by month 6; no retention programme proven.
  • Budget brief: stated ₹30 L a month vs ₹20.3 L measured Meta plus Google; if ₹10 L runs elsewhere true CAC is ₹1,700+.
  • Sixteen of the 42 register issues sit outside the ad account (checkout, product mix, finance, data).

III.2

Tests and what they taught

Not every test worked. Each one changed the next decision.

  1. Hypothesis

    A bigger brand budget captures more cheap brand demand.

  2. Action

    Raised Search | Brand | Bangalore from ₹2,000 to ₹3,100 a day on 17 Aug, against own spec cap of ₹150-200 (brand demand ~₹64 a day). Campaign sat on Smart Bidding, so ad-group caps did nothing.

  3. Result

    ₹57,249 spent, 23.77 conversions, ₹2,408 per conversion vs ₹3,093 for 84.98 conversions (₹36) on Brand_3june. Broad match bought non-brand clicks.

  4. Learning

    Budget follows measured demand. Check bid strategy before launch. Caught 24 Aug.

  5. Next decision

    Pause Brand Broad 01, one brand campaign with exact CPC cap ₹8-12, broad to phrase, pause 22 dead postcode rows (handed over; outcome not recorded).

  1. Hypothesis

    Google-reported conversion value is reliable enough to scale budgets on.

  2. Action

    Scaled in August: Sales_PMax_CatchAll ₹6,087 to ₹38,806 (+537.5%), Stand_14Jan ₹1,73,483 to ₹2,60,342 (+50.1%); Google billed +143.3%.

  3. Result

    Both campaigns carried defective value (Stand_14Jan ₹12.33 Cr on 1-4 Aug). Shopify: gross sales +21.8%, orders +16.1%, sessions +53.3%, conversion -24.2%; incremental return 2.99x gross, 2.24x net; gross MER 19.61x to 9.82x. S_Tofu scale-up (+268.7%, CPC ₹4.67) was sound.

  4. Learning

    Reconcile Google purchases to Shopify weekly; stop if Google claims more than 60% of orders.

  5. Next decision

    Hold September spend flat at ₹9.0-9.5 L; fix mix and reversals first; scale only above ~45% gross margin (unknown).

  1. Hypothesis

    Purchase tracking is broken: only 33 lifetime add-to-cart events (P0).

  2. Action

    Carried the figure through four documents; planned a Shopify pixel rebuild and a 7 Sep scaling hard-stop on it.

  3. Result

    False alarm: goal cards count primary actions only; add-to-cart fired 26,737 times. Real defects: add-to-cart value bug (GA4 control ₹5,819 per cart), purchase counted twice (₹4.74 Cr claimed vs ₹2.98 Cr real, 1.6x), no primary action on two goals (35% of 1-2 Sep conversions not sales).

  4. Learning

    Read conversion actions, not goal tiles. Cost: a week of gated decisions. Caught 2 Sep.

  5. Next decision

    Purchase CAPI out of Conversions, add-to-cart no value, one live test order, 7-day reconciliation, then value net of reversals. Do not promote add-to-cart to primary.

  1. Hypothesis

    Cart remarketing recovers abandoned carts.

  2. Action

    Launched Demand Gen C04, cart 0-7 day audience, three ads, with no stop-or-continue date; second ad group specced on the same base.

  3. Result

    By 2 Sep: ₹11,971, 3,118 clicks, 0 purchases; 92 view-through conversions argued for patience.

  4. Learning

    Set the stop date before launch. Cost ₹11,971.

  5. Next decision

    Two more weeks with corrected cart audience and a conversion action attached; all-conversions check pending (open at close).

  1. Hypothesis

    No local action was primary, so store bidding was blind.

  2. Action

    Set local actions as the primary conversion for PMax Store, 21-25 Aug.

  3. Result

    CPC ₹38.60 to ₹13.13 (-66%), CTR 0.69% to 2.40%, clicks 122 to 1,241, cost per local action ₹38.24.

  4. Learning

    Highest-value single edit of the engagement. The 34.06% rate counts directions and calls, not sales.

  5. Next decision

    Fund Bridal and Festive asset groups before 6 Nov (Dhanteras, Diwali); keep local actions Secondary outside PMax Store.

  1. Hypothesis

    A 17-day July can be scaled linearly to a month.

  2. Action

    Blended review scaled July by 31/17.

  3. Result

    July MER published at 3.20x, restated to 2.68x the same day: days 1-17 carry 66.1% of a month's net revenue because returns post on days 18-31.

  4. Learning

    Never linear-scale a month. The Jul 2026 workbook point remains an estimate.

  5. Next decision

    Use full months only; request monthly returns report.
