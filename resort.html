The case in eight lines

Context
A 12-acre resort with 59 rooms, two pools and a 2,000-guest lawn. Six products on one site: day-outs and rooms bring volume; weddings, offsites and celebrations bring value per booking.
Problem
Every product ends in "Enquire" and waits for a human. No online payment. Ads and Meta run, but no tag or pixel connects them to a booking.
Evidence
43-page site crawl, Lighthouse, Google Hotels and reseller listings, Google Ads Transparency Center and Meta Ad Library, Google Maps across 17 resorts within 20 km, Business Profile performance Apr–Sep 2026.
Diagnosis
Revenue leaks where guests try to commit: price, booking, payment, confirmation. Some of the biggest fixes are operational, not marketing.
Action
Manager access to the Business Profile on 28 Sep; 45 changes live the same day. A 30-day plan that fixes conversion and pricing before any extra ad spend.
Outcome
Data unavailable. Work started on 28 September 2026.
Limitations
No access yet to ad accounts, budgets, OTA contracts, commissions or booking volumes. Rank checks are signed-in snapshots.
Next step
Sell day-outs and rooms online with payment, one rate card everywhere, tag every lead to its source, and find the lost calls.

03

How I found it

Followed a guest from a Google search to a paid booking, on a phone, and priced the same product everywhere it was listed.

The same product, three prices

Direct prices exclude GST; third-party prices as displayed for a 3–4 Oct stay, captured 25 Sep. The ₹499 day-out listing is live but its booking flow wasn't tested. Measured

Search demand goes to aggregators

Top 10 on 2 of 14 target searches. 54 of 84 top-six slots held by OTAs, aggregators and directories. Zero top-10 spots for "day outing", "resorts in bangalore" or "wedding resort".

Signed-in snapshot, 25 Sep · Measured

12 conversion checks on the site

5 pass, 4 partial, 3 fail. Fails: no book-now, no online payment, no urgency. Partial: price consistency, a 9+ field form, reviews, photos. Mobile LCP 7.6 s in the lab.

Ads can't see revenue

Google runs local, Performance Max-style and video ads; Meta runs wedding, birthday and corporate. No Google Ads conversion tag on the site, no Meta Pixel. One Meta ad sends traffic to Facebook, not the site.

04

Diagnosis

Don't start with more ad spend. Fix conversion, pricing and tracking first, then scale paid media on real bookings.

Pricing control, not competition

Competitors sit in a normal spread around the resort's direct prices. The problem is the resort's own inventory showing cheaper on third-party sites. A guest who sees ₹1,780 on an OTA and ₹3,999 direct books through the OTA, and the resort pays commission on a booking it had already won.

Calls are leaking, not demand

People still find the profile and still drive there. They stopped calling. To check in order: the line going unanswered on weekends, enquiries moving to WhatsApp, or Meta lead forms absorbing the high-intent audience.

Not a marketing problem: the rating

720 one- and two-star reviews repeat the same themes: leaking bathrooms, pool upkeep, slow food service at events. Replies buy goodwill. Only fixing operations moves 3.9 toward 4.2.

A review pattern Google may punish

In the latest 100 reviews, about 24 five-star ratings came from 0–1 review accounts, several in one week with identical sub-scores. If incentivised or staff-written, Google can strip them in bulk and restrict the profile. Flagged to the owner.

05

What I changed

Business Profile, 28 September 2026. 45 changes in one session.

Categories +4

Conference centre, swimming pool, buffet restaurant, adventure sports. Opens the profile to searches it was invisible for.

Under Google review

Services 0 → 17

Each with a plain description and a "from" price where one is published: day outing ₹1,499, corporate offsite ₹1,999, Sunday brunch, wedding lawns, banquet hall, stays from ₹3,999 and more.

Every recent review answered

20 unanswered reviews in the latest 100, including five one- and two-star. Specific replies, a phone number and a request for the visit date on complaints. 80% → 100% replied.

Posts that link to the right page

Three posts on products the last 20 never covered (pool day, conference venue, banquet hall), each linked to its package page with UTM tags so GA4 can count them.

Held back on purpose

The name and address fix: any edit can trigger re-verification and hide a 5,400-review listing for days. Scheduled for when someone on site can complete a video check.

07

Open questions for management

Each answer changes the plan.

Distribution

Which OTA and reseller contracts are active? What commission does each take, and what net rate reaches the resort? Are lower rates deliberate? Who updates them? Is there a channel manager?

Operations

Is the main phone line answered at 11 am, 4 pm and 8 pm on a Saturday? Who confirms bookings, and how fast? Are tennis, airport shuttle and free breakfast actually offered as listed?

08

What I'd do next

The first 30 days.

Sell online, with payment

Date and guest picker, UPI or card deposit, instant WhatsApp ticket, record into the CRM with its value. Start with day-outs: the simplest product to sell without a call.

One rate card, one owner

Channel manager so OTA rates move together, weekly parity check on Google Hotels, a best-rate-direct promise on the site, and the official site listed in Google Hotel Center.

Tag every lead to its ad

Google Ads tag and Meta Pixel on booking and enquiry events; booking value written back so ads optimise to revenue, not clicks.

A review engine after every visit

WhatsApp thank-you two hours after checkout. 4–5★ get a one-tap Google link; 1–3★ go to the manager first. Target 15 genuine reviews a week.

Estimate 272 more five-star reviews lift the average to 4.0, assuming no new low ratings
