Leads hit an all-time high. So why did the average bill keep falling, and where did the leads go after the form?

The case in eight lines

Context
16 premium salons across Bengaluru malls and high streets. Revenue is booked at the till; Google and Meta drive enquiries, calls, WhatsApp chats and walk-ins.
Problem
February 2026 was the worst month in a 20-month record: ₹1.91 Cr, 8,084 bills. Google ran without store-level structure; Meta wasn't run per salon at all.
Evidence
Google Ads exports Jan 2025–Aug 2026, Meta Ads Manager, salon point-of-sale sheets, auction insights, search terms, demographics.
Diagnosis
Demand was under-built and bought expensively. Once fixed, the leaks moved downstream: two campaigns wasting spend, no lead-to-bill visibility, and a falling average bill.
Action
Store-level Google rebuild (25 campaigns), Meta per salon (26), new website domain, Android booking app, salon-level P&L joining spend to till.
Outcome
Mar–Aug 2026 revenue ₹16.10 Cr vs ₹14.04 Cr a year earlier (+14.7%). Bills +21.6%. Every month beat the same month of 2025.
Limitations
No lead-to-booking data. New stores opened in 2026. The revenue rise is not causally attributed to media. Cost per bill rose.
Next step
5-minute WhatsApp callback per store, tracking repair, and an average-bill programme on the salon floor.
01

What was happening

I joined on 28 February 2026, in the weakest month on record.

The business sells haircuts, colour, treatments and packages from 16 salons. Most revenue is decided in the chair, not online. Marketing's job is to fill chairs at each salon, every day.

Through 2025 Google paid ₹10–14 a click with click-through under 1%. There was no store-level campaign structure and no Meta activity per salon. Reporting stopped at clicks.

04

Diagnosis

By August, demand was no longer the constraint. Money was leaking in three places the ad dashboard couldn't see.

1 · Spend concentrated where it returned least

Demand Gen (₹1,36,752 for 173 conversions, ₹790 each) and Koramangala 4th PMax (₹1,27,776) took 34.8% of Google spend for 7.9% of output.

Allocation leak · Measured

2 · No path from lead to chair

28,144 leads across Google and Meta in August, but Business Profile actions and the CRM call log weren't connected. Nobody could say how many leads became bills, or how fast anyone called back.

Lead-to-booking leak · Data unavailable

3 · Average bill below last year

₹2,813 vs ₹2,983 in August 2025. At August volume that gap is ₹17.78 L a month, more than the whole ad budget. Packages were 28.1% of sales, retail product 5.4%. This is a salon-floor problem: menu, packages, stylist conversation.

Not a marketing problem · Calculated: (₹2,983 − ₹2,813) × 10,459 bills

4 · A conversion spike nobody could explain

6–12 August reported a 19.3% conversion rate; the other 24 days averaged 1.7%. Click-through rose with it, so it may be real. Or a conversion action changed. Most of August's 22,532 conversions sit in those seven days. I flagged it before anyone else found it.

Measurement leak · Measured, cause unresolved at exit

05

What I changed

Five workstreams. Media is the visible part.

Google Ads, rebuilt per store

One demand unit per salon plus three always-on network campaigns. Search, Performance Max and Demand Gen. Intent-tiered keyword lists and negatives; competitor and off-geo suggestions rejected. Launches for Nexus Vega City and Lavelle Road.

25 campaigns live · naming: [Type]_[Store]_[Intent]_[Year]

Meta, per salon, from zero

Lead forms per salon from May, network-wide in June. Click-to-WhatsApp and click-to-call alongside. 132 Instagram boosts built.

26 campaigns · ₹31.74 per result in August, down from about ₹50

Web and tracking

New storefront domain, moved from Shopify to Netlify, plus a single-store site for Lavelle Road. Conversion tracking on the new domain started but not confirmed at exit.

2 domains

Android booking app

Jetpack Compose, 6-step booking, encrypted login, bookings synced to a Google Sheets CRM. Reached Play closed testing.

v1.0.1 · not in production

Reporting that reaches the till

Monthly report issues, a 20-month demand-to-till series every figure reconciles to, and the first salon-by-salon P&L joining ad spend to point-of-sale revenue. A 19-item gap register, each finding traceable to a file.

Where August's extra spend went

Total invested rose from ₹5.47 L in July to ₹10.32 L. Meta scaled while its lead cost fell 37%; the two orange bars are the waste. Measured

What the growth does not prove. Two stores (Nexus Vega City, Lavelle Road) launched in 2026, so part of the year-on-year gain may be new capacity rather than marketing. Excluding March, the handover month, April–August still shows +16.0% revenue and +21.4% bills. Average bill fell 5.7% over the six months, so growth came from more customers, not bigger tickets.

07

What failed, and what blocked me

Hypothesis, action, result, learning.

Demand Gen as a prospecting layer

Hypothesis

Video and image prospecting adds demand Search can't reach.

Action

Two Demand Gen campaigns, ₹1,36,752 in August.

Result

173 conversions at ₹790, against ₹33.71 for the account.

Learning

Switch off. Move the money to store PMax and Search. No version of it was working.

Scaling Koramangala 4th Block

Hypothesis

A small salon under target needs more demand.

Action

PMax spend up 89% month on month.

Result

₹1,53,007 all-in for ₹7,45,995 of billing: 4.9×, ₹454 of media per bill.

Learning

Cap at ₹60,000 and rebuild the asset group. Its target was the second-smallest in the estate.

Instagram boosts

Hypothesis

Boosting organic posts builds cheap reach per salon.

Action

132 boosts built.

Result

59 never delivered. 51 were optimised to profile visits, a view, not a booking.

Learning

Fix the scheduling handoff; switch the objective to leads or WhatsApp.

Blocked · Booking app

Google Play requires 12+ testers on a closed track for 14 days before production. Not completed by exit.

Blocked · Lead-to-bill tracking

Business Profile actions and the CRM call log were never connected. Without them no report could prove which leads became customers.

Cost of scale

Cost per MQL rose from ₹17.13 (Feb) to ₹33.71 (Aug). Blended revenue per Google rupee fell from 96.5× to 63.5×. Volume was bought; some of it cheaply, some not.

08

What I'd do next

Handed over on 16 September, ranked by rupees per hour of effort.

Week 1 · Settle the 6–12 August window

One hour in the conversions report. Until then August's ₹33.71 cost per lead isn't a planning number.

Reallocate, don't cut

Hold ₹10.3 L. Kill Demand Gen, cap Koramangala 4th, move ₹2.0 L to the eight salons under target and Mantri Avenue.

Modelled September ₹2.62 Cr vs ₹2.53 Cr if the mix is left alone · Estimate

Month 2 · Build the callback loop

Every lead to the right salon's WhatsApp within five minutes. Leads are at record volume; speed is where they're lost.

Take the average bill back to ₹2,983

Menu pricing, packages, stylist conversation. Worth ₹17.78 L a month at August volume, and it isn't a media lever.
