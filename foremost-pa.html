II.2

What the history shows

Patterns found by re-reading the full history, not just the months I ran. Each is labelled: measured pattern, correlation, anomaly or seasonality.

Anomaly

The $118 account CPL excludes $3,648 of spend

Three live campaigns spent $5,667 for 48 leads = $118.06. The account-total row shows $9,315. $9,315 / 48 = $194.06. The headline $118 is the live-campaign figure, not the account figure. Jun 1-17 spend ($9,315 - $8,320 = $995, $58.53/day) fits the $60/day cap, so $9,315 and $8,320 look like one spend series; leads do not reconcile (56 vs 48).

The numbers

Campaigns: $3,916 + $1,607 + $144 = $5,667. Account total $9,315. Gap $3,648 (39%). 5,667/48 = $118.06. 9,315/48 = $194.06. Clicks 266+129+37 = 432; 48/432 = 11.11% (matches table). Jun 1-17: $995 / 17 = $58.53 per day vs $60 budget.

So whatQuote CPL with its base. Spend in the 14 paused campaigns carries no lead count in any source. True account-wide CPL is unknown.

Measured pattern

Foremost has the lowest top-of-page rate of all six advertisers

The report headline says Foremost wins 62% of auctions it enters. The metric is top-of-page rate. At 62.04% it sits below all five rivals (65.97% to 86.35%). Rivals appear above Foremost 37.5% to 85.4% of shared auctions. Foremost outranks them only 21-22% of the time.

The numbers

Top of page: stellar 86.35, recovery-claim 81.49, fightforyourclaim 73.60, alphapia 68.02, yourinsuranceattorney 65.97, Foremost 62.04. Outranking share 21.29-22.45%. Position above 37.54-85.40%.

So whatThe ad is not 'strong when it shows'. Bid and rank are weak as well as budget. The 8 Jul audit separately notes Foremost trails 2 of 3 tracked rivals on generic absolute top rate.

Measured pattern

Desktop gap is real. The May drop and Jan-Apr gain are noise.

Desktop cost 4 x $397.95 = $1,592, 28% of $5,667 spend, for 4 of 48 leads. That CVR gap is statistically clear. May vs April (3 fewer leads) and Jan vs April are not significant at 95%.

The numbers

Desktop 4/147 clicks (2.72%) vs mobile 43/276 (15.58%): z = 4.0, p < 0.001. Apr 15/146 vs May 12/160: z = 0.85, p = 0.39. Jan 9/159 vs Apr: z = 1.49, p = 0.13. Feb 7/157 vs Apr: z = 1.95, p = 0.051.

So whatPresent the device fix as proven. Present 'May regression' and 'CVR doubled' as unproven: 5 months and 56 leads is too thin.

Anomaly

The brand name drew 46 impressions. Rivals' names drew 1,806.

The paused brand keyword earned 46 impressions and 10 clicks in its lifetime, at $5.66 a click. The audit expects branded CPC of $0.50-2.50. Foremost's own broad keywords showed 1,806 impressions on 'stellar public adjusting', 39x its own name.

The numbers

$56.55 / 10 clicks = $5.66 CPC. 1,806 / 46 = 39.3x. Competitor term spend $122.31. $150-300 per month at $5.66 buys 27-53 clicks vs 10 lifetime.

So whatBrand search demand looks small. The audit's 'highest-ROI fix' rests on generic benchmarks, not this account. Size the brand budget after a live check.

Measured pattern

Two keywords took 66% of Local & Near Me spend

'public claims adjuster near me' ($533.48, 1 lead) and 'pub. adj. for home ins. claim' (~$526.68, 2 leads) cost $1,060 of the campaign's $1,607. Without them the campaign runs at ~$91 CPL, not $178.51.

The numbers

$533.48 + $526.68 = $1,060.16 (66% of $1,607; 3 of 9 leads). Remainder $546.84 / 6 leads = $91.14.

So whatCampaign-level CPL hid a two-keyword leak. Hindsight view; it needs checking against live search terms before claiming savings.

II.3

What ads can't fix, and what the data can't prove

Not a marketing problem

What the data shows ads cannot fix. Saying so early saves budget.

  • Brand campaign was paused during an account restructure. Who made the restructure is not stated; if it was the HTS-Jun10 build, the gap may be self-inflicted.
  • Claim intake, close rate and fee are outside the data. Revenue lags leads by 3-12 months per README.
  • Hurricane season Jun-Nov shifts demand. Seasonality is not separable from the CPL trend with 5 months of data.

Data not available

What would prove more, and does not exist in the sources yet.

  • Signed claims, settlements, fees. No CRM data.
  • Lead definition (form vs call). No call tracking; CallRail only recommended.
  • My start date and what I changed directly in the account.
  • Whether the brand rebuild, negatives, desktop modifier and mobile form went live.
  • June and July monthly series. Monthly table stops at May.
  • Leads and spend for the 14 paused campaigns ($3,648 gap).
  • Cause of May CVR change. GA4 and session recordings not provided.
  • Core Web Vitals. Quality Score. Locations report. Live SERP check.
  • Source inconsistencies to fix before reuse: projection model shows 22 leads/month vs '45 historical (90-day)' (15/month), assumes $2,700/month vs live $60/day and Jan-May $1,664 average, uses $122 CPL and 95% mobile vs measured $118/$148.57 and 89.6%; README says 79% of leads in Miami-Dade and Broward but the geo table gives 34 of 48 = 70.8%; June organic impressions 296 (5.4% of May paid impressions) at average position 25.
